AI Security InstitutesAISIs

Full method

Qualifying body

Qualifying body: an act of a national government or of an international organisation, such as the EU, either (a) designates the body as its AI safety or AI security institute in the sense of the International Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation and Science (formerly the International Network of AI Safety Institutes), as its member institution in that Network, or as its counterpart to other AI safety institutes, provided the same act gives it an AI safety or model-evaluation remit (a body, not a ministry desk); or (b) assigns it, as a public function, the evaluation or testing of general-purpose, foundation or frontier AI models for their capabilities or for risks arising from the model's own capabilities or behaviour, such as misuse potential, harmful outputs or loss of control. Assignment means a constitutive or mandate decision, or an authorised announcement of an ongoing public responsibility. A research programme, funding line, testing schedule, rename, government support or recognition, the state's Network membership, or the body's own description is not an assignment. Title, legal form, newness and size do not matter. These do not qualify a body on their own: certifying products or deployed systems, conformity assessment, market surveillance (including AI Act authorities; Spain's planned institute inside AESIA shows how such an authority can host one), securing AI systems against attack (for example prompt-injection hardening or security certification), auditing fairness, bias, explainability or trustworthiness, performance benchmarking, general AI research or R&D networks, promotion and sandboxes.

Ladder: each jurisdiction's status is the strongest act in force concerning a qualifying body, dated from that act.

Established: an official text says the body exists or has been designated. A body announced as established counts before it operates; known activity is reported separately.

Planned: an adopted commitment to create or designate a named qualifying body, through a cabinet or council decision, an adopted strategy or plan naming it, an enacted budget line, or a statement by the head of government or responsible minister.

Proposed: the government's own formal proposal awaiting approval, submitted to cabinet or parliament or published by the responsible ministry for formal consultation.

Exploring: the government studies the option of a qualifying body or promises a report on it; an internal or working draft names one; or a government-appointed advisory commission publishes a recommendation that itself names a qualifying body or function. Generic AI institutes, centres or councils, and exploring a lab, network or mechanism without a qualifying remit, do not count. Parliamentary and independent inquiries do not count. Rejection, withdrawal, lapse or supersession returns the state to 'None identified' unless another act qualifies; silence is not rejection. Signing an international statement such as the Seoul statement of intent, or joining the Network, does not count by itself.

Dates and precision: use a year-only date when no exact date is published. If the creation date is not published, use only the precision the sources support and state the limit. A record may use the first retrieved official confirmation, clearly identified as confirmation rather than the founding date.

Why this bar

Why testing, not size: testing general-purpose or frontier AI models is what distinguishes an AISI from AI regulators and digital ministries, and it is the work members of the international network do together. Budgets and staff differ too much between countries for a size threshold to work, so we report them separately, together with whether published work is credited to the institute.

How each state was checked

How each state was checked: 'individual' means the sources for that state were searched and read one by one; 'broad search' means it was covered only by searches across many countries, so 'None identified' is weaker evidence there.

Population of European states

Population of European states: Eurostat, 1 January 2025. 'Under 10 million' on the page means fewer than 10 million people.

Missing values

Missing values: an omitted field or null means not known or not published, never zero or false. For budget and staff, Not published means that no figure was found in the sources checked; these searches are not yet logged with dates, unlike those for testing and access.

Population outside the 32 European states

Population outside the 32 European states: World Bank, latest available year; Taiwan: its Ministry of the Interior.

Regions: the UN's standard country grouping (M49). The UN does not list Taiwan separately, so we place it in Eastern Asia.

Known activity

Known activity: assessed separately from government status and shown only for established institutes. Published work is a published evaluation, test result, benchmark or technical report credited to the institute by name, including named organisational contributions and documented predecessors. Joint testing is named participation in a testing exercise; an exercise counts as joint testing even when the institute also authors its report. Guidance alone, policy or programme reports, country-only or host-agency credit, and staff affiliations alone do not qualify. Tools, datasets and benchmarks qualify only as evaluation artefacts. Every output records its qualification and reason. Each kind uses its earliest qualifying institute-credit date, keeping the recorded precision and citing that item. No qualifying work means none was found. Absence is not evidence of inactivity.

Freshness

Freshness: the recorded check date is the date of the last full re-check of a record. Reading some sources, checking identifiers or adding excerpts does not advance it. Partial work is recorded separately with its date and scope. A historical date whose original full-check log is unavailable is marked as a historical upper bound; it does not guarantee freshness.

Rescinded or replaced instruments

Rescinded or replaced instruments: records describe the current state. A fact that rests on a rescinded or replaced instrument never appears as current. History is kept only where the instrument is itself a recorded act (a timeline event, legal basis, name or agreement), dated with the date it was rescinded and the act that rescinded it, both sourced. Context or supporting notes resting on a replaced instrument are removed, not annotated. Sources that are legal instruments carry the date their status was last verified. Record checks re-verify it, and the release check warns when a cited instrument's status was last verified more than six months before release.

Bills

Bills: a record states a bill's latest formal step with its date. It does not say that a bill proposes something or that a bill is dead unless a vote or a withdrawal is quoted.

Status confidence

Status confidence: a measure of evidence, not of disagreement over classification. High means a primary source states the decisive act; medium means indirect primary evidence or several independent secondary accounts; low means a single secondary source. For a finding of no institute, medium requires an individual check with recorded queries in English and a national or official language, plus at least one retrieved primary source showing the state's AI governance set-up, consistent with no qualifying institute. English meets both language requirements where it is official; the language note records the languages searched and coverage limits. The governance citations and note identify the reviewed governance evidence. English-only searches where English is not official, secondary-only evidence and other incomplete checks remain low. High also requires a dated official primary statement, no more than 12 calendar months before the dataset date, that explicitly says no institute exists or is planned. Medium has no fixed age limit; the original search and full-check dates show freshness. This finding is limited to our search; it is not proof of absence.

Excerpts

Excerpts: every positive status, status date, legal basis, budget amount, staff figure, first output, agreement, head, reporting line, partner relation and partner how sentence needs a short quotation that carries the claim. Other fields have excerpts where possible. Each gloss translates only its own excerpt and adds no facts. Each quotation is linked to the values it supports; a check confirms each link exists, and review judges whether the quotation supports the value.

Primary evidence for status changes

Primary evidence for status changes: every timeline act that moves a status (exploring, proposed, planned, established, a withdrawal or a closure) and every launch date cites a primary source, and an excerpt from a primary source carries the act and its date. A primary source published on the act's date carries that date. A press report, feed item or aggregator can lead to an act but does not carry it alone; where no primary text could be retrieved, the event keeps the secondary citation, its access note says what was tried, and the event is listed as an accepted exception.

Outputs

Outputs: a publication, tool or dataset counts when it is written by the institute or credited to it as its own work, including named organisational contributions. Publishing or hosting alone does not count when the institute acts only as a regulator or facilitator for work drafted by others. Commissioned work published as the institute's own publication counts, with the research author identified. Staff co-authorship or a link in a staff bibliography alone does not count. Host-agency and government documents count only when the institute is named as author. Translations of other bodies' documents do not count. Drafts, versions, summaries and translations within one document lineage form one item, with the latest title and the earliest public date. Documented former names and institutional predecessors follow the same credit rule; their inclusion does not backdate government designation. Every catalogue output records an explicit institutional credit identity and date. The catalogue date is shared across a document lineage; the credit date records the earliest public version for which the institute’s credit is supported. If institute credit first appears in a later version, its credit date is later than the lineage date. An institute's own later post does not backdate that credit to an earlier staff-only paper. An explicit later official statement that names the institute as author of the earlier version, or credits it as the institute's own work, can establish credit for that version; a statement that it only published or hosted the work cannot. For arXiv papers, the recorded first-submission date is used as the bibliographic version date; it does not show the exact day the paper became publicly available. Cite the artefact itself where it can be retrieved.

First output

First output: the earliest qualifying date credited to the institute by name, including network reports that name it. Use the explicitly recorded institutional credit date. It is not limited to evaluations. Country or host-agency participation alone does not name an institute.

Intervals

Intervals between milestones are computed from recorded dates, never entered by hand. They are counted in signed whole days: from announcement to establishment, from establishment to the first qualifying activity, and from announcement to the first qualifying activity. Announcement is read from the commitment and proposal events in the timeline, establishment from the designations that have not been withdrawn. A designation is never read as an announcement, so a state designated without an earlier recorded commitment or proposal has no announcement interval. The first qualifying activity is the earliest institute-credit date of any output that qualifies as Known activity, including work credited to documented predecessors. It dates the first documented qualifying credit, which can be later than the work itself; participants in one joint exercise share that date when their credit begins with the same public version. It is not the generated first output, which also counts guidance and other outputs that do not qualify. The activity intervals are given only while the institute is established, even where qualifying work is credited to a planned or proposed body. A month-only or year-only date is read as every day of that month or year up to the dataset date, never as a midpoint, so the interval is a range from its shortest to its longest reading. Where several records could mark a milestone, it runs from the earliest day any of them can mean to the earliest day by which one of them certainly happened. A negative interval means the later milestone came first; a range from a negative to a positive value means the order is not known. An interval is empty when either date is not recorded. The intervals do not measure speed or quality. The announcing act and the kind of body differ between states, for example a rename of an existing body, a federation of existing bodies, a new agency, or one legal act with separate adoption and entry-into-force dates.

News lists material developments

News lists material developments: changes of status, changes of mandate or name, budget decisions for the institute itself, and agreements. A budget decision appears only where its date is known. Individual publications and changes of head are not listed.

Agreements

Agreements: the institute must be a named party to the agreement, or a government agreement must name it as an implementing body. A signed agreement requires a signatory; an announced agreement must identify the intended institute party or implementing role. A pledge records a company commitment announced by the government for the institute or its documented predecessor, with no signed agreement established; a recipient is the beneficiary of that commitment. Only pledges record recipients. States describe the recorded act, not proof that its original terms remain in force. Where an agreement names possible collaboration, the recorded scope keeps that condition. Purely domestic inter-agency memorandums do not count. The counterparty type institute is used only for peer AI Security Institutes and the EU AI Office; other research organisations get the type other.

Annual funding

Annual funding: every amount with a stated funding year or period has an annual amount and an explanation of its basis, including supplementary, proposed, related-body and press-reported amounts. Keep the commitment, scope and confidence label; an annual figure is not proof of adoption or expenditure. Use the allocation for the fiscal year containing the dataset date when a yearly profile is published. Otherwise use the mean over a stated multi-year duration, or the nominal amount for a single stated funding year, and explain its basis. One-off supplementary amounts belong to their funding year and are not treated as recurring spending. The annual basis distinguishes current-year allocations, period means and other figures. A launch or announcement year alone is not a funding period; do not invent an annual figure when no period is stated. For comparison, the reference year is the calendar year of the dataset date. Compare only a whole-institute annual figure, adopted or announced, whose funding period overlaps the reference year; one-off amounts are never compared. Among several, prefer the period containing the dataset date, then the latest earlier period, then the nearest later period; for equal periods prefer adopted, then the latest decision. Proposals and periods outside the reference year are never compared. Components are never compared alone. A published annual allocation takes precedence over a period mean for the same date. Component quotations support the inputs to a calculated total, not a quotation of the result. Decisions dated wholly after the dataset date are excluded from comparison; partial decision dates keep their recorded precision.

AI Act authorities

AI Act authorities: an act in force takes precedence over an inconsistent Commission list. Every EU or EEA authority record also cites the Commission list. The designation basis records a law, decree, government decision, cabinet decision or ministerial act; where no national designation act is identified, the basis is recorded as not yet identified. A pending proposal is not a designation in force. For the listed uses, Article 74(8) requires every member state to designate either its competent data protection supervisory authorities or another authority that meets the stated independence conditions; it does not automatically designate data protection authorities in all member states.

Leadership

Leadership: a head is stated only when an official channel of the institute, its host or its government names the person in the role, such as its website, a government directory or an official social account. Press reports alone do not qualify. Otherwise the row reads not published; not applicable is used only where no institute exists. The role title is the exact published one; if none is published, the role is recorded as lead with a note that no formal title is published. The head is the top executive by published title, the chair excluded, and "Acting" and "part-time" are kept as published. The newest official source wins and a conflict is noted. A historical appointment or signature does not by itself confirm continuing tenure.

Reporting line

Reporting line: the body an institute reports to is the ministry-level body the sources place it under: one above an agency, university or research host, and the host itself when the host is a ministry. Several bodies are joined with "; ". The excerpt carries the source's own wording and nothing is inferred. Where no text names a parent, the row is not published.

National partners

National partners: a partner is a domestic public body or public-interest research organisation that a primary text of the institute or its government names as working with the institute on its mission. The host, funders of the host, private firms, catch-all groups and the reports-to body are never entries. A consortium is one entry, and its members are listed individually only with separate evidence. Member is used only when the institute is a federation of existing bodies and the text lists the body as a constituent. Works with is used when a text in force assigns the body a specific task with the institute; an adopted programme for the current period that assigns the work counts as in force. Planned is used when the text is a plan, strategy or future commitment. A body named only as an example in a general duty to cooperate is not entered. The cyber agency flag marks the national cyber security agency or the body containing it; when no entry has the flag, the notes name the cyber agency searched and the date, and no other absence is ever claimed. The what field says in two to six words what the body is, from its own page or the quote. The how field is one sentence of at most 25 words, written from the bound quote alone, like a gloss. Every stated value, relation and how is bound to a verbatim excerpt; not published needs the dated search log. Where a card names only some partners, it names them in this order: cyber agency first, then members, works with, planned, then in authored order; every partner is shown on the page with how it works with the institute, and is in the downloads and the API.

Testing capacity and model access

Testing capacity and model access: each institute records whether a source states that it tests AI models and how it gets access to them, using only the defined codes. Every value other than not published needs a verbatim excerpt from a retrieved source in which the institute, or a body explicitly acting for it, is the subject. Never infer a value from press narrative, a parent ministry's powers, a partner's general expertise or network membership. Testing capacity may hold one or both modes.

In house: a source reports testing or evaluation of AI models that the institute itself has carried out or is carrying out, such as published results, a network report crediting it with running tests, or an official statement that it is already testing. A statement of role, function or focus does not count. For a federation, only testing that a source attributes to the institute counts, never a member body's own general track record. A joint network testing report counts as the institute's own testing only if it says the institute ran tests, not only analysis, translation checks or task design, and the institute existed at that date.

Via other bodies: a source states that a named body outside the institute, such as a contractor or partner lab, tests or evaluates models for it. Model access records what sources state, and its value is derived from that, never set by hand. An access event is dated access by the institute to a model before its public release, or deeper than the public gets, such as reduced or removed safeguards, weights or internals. Testing through a public interface is not an event, and testing alone shows access only if the source says the developer gave it. An event's date is the model's public release date, unless the date of the access is published. Either way it is the date by which access had happened, so the access took place on or before it. An event counts when the model is in Epoch AI's frontier set, which Epoch AI defines as the models in the top 5 by training compute at the time of their release, or when its developer was a leading developer at the event date. A leading developer has had a model in Epoch AI's frontier set released since 1 January 2023, and a model in Epoch AI's notable models released in the twelve months before the event. Both are cited from Epoch AI's data. Access to other models goes in the notes. A legal power is an operative provision, named by act and article, that gives the institute, or explicitly the institute on behalf of its authority, a power to require access to models, with its conditions. An explainer is not enough; the provision itself is quoted. A developer agreement is a published agreement between the institute and a developer with a model in Epoch AI's frontier set that provides for access to, or evaluation of, the developer's models. The value is Yes when the latest event falls within the twelve months up to the data date, where a date known only to the month or year counts from its first possible day. It is Yes, evidence from the year of the latest event when that event is older; otherwise it is By law with a legal power, Access agreed with a developer agreement, and None found with a dated search log. Planned, for testing capacity when no mode is stated: an adopted text, such as a cabinet decision, an adopted plan, strategy or guidelines or a government action plan, sets it up in the future. A proposal, such as a consultation draft, a budget proposal not yet voted or a bill, gives not published, with the proposal quoted in the notes with its source and date.

Not published: searched and no source states it; a dated search log records each query, its language and the sources read. Where a newer official source contradicts an older one, the newer one decides and the notes record both. Notes also record explicit negative statements, such as no own staff, and joint network testing as testing reported with its date. Not published means not stated in the sources searched, never that the institute lacks the capacity or access.

Technical staff

Technical staff: the technical count is filled whenever a source states the number of staff in technical roles, such as researchers, engineers or evaluators, or states a share of a stated total. An exact count or an explicit lower bound that covers only part of the institute, such as one lab or unit, is entered as at least, and the description names the part. A share of a stated total for the whole institute, such as about half of 25 staff, is entered as about, rounded to the nearest whole number with halves rounded down, and the description says that the count is derived and from which figures. An exact share of an exact count for part of the institute, such as half of a lab's 20 staff, is entered as at least the derived count, and the description names the part and says that the count is derived and from which figures. For part of the institute, approximate counts, upper bounds and approximate shares give no count; the description records them. A share is a fraction or a percentage; words such as most or many give no count. A count is never derived from a total alone.

Staffing model

Staffing model: each institute records how a source says it is staffed, using only the defined codes; dated headcounts stay in the staff figures. Every value other than not published needs a verbatim excerpt from a retrieved source in which the institute, or a body explicitly acting for it, is the subject, and only a source published in 2023 or later supports it; a page without a date counts as of the day it was read, and older sources go in the notes as history. Never infer a value from a legal form, a host body's own staff, a partner's expertise or press narrative. The first code that applies, in this order, is used.

Staff of existing bodies: a federation of existing bodies whose staff do its work; used only for that legal form.

Core team with partners: partner institutions do the institute's research or testing under its programme, and any central team of its own only coordinates, sets policy or represents it.

Unit in a host body: the institute is a unit of an agency, public research body or university, never a ministry, government department or the European Commission, and a source states that the institute's staff are the host's employees, are assigned or seconded from the host or are hired through the host's own recruitment, or that an existing unit of the host was designated as the institute.

Own staff: the institute employs or recruits people in posts of its own, including as a unit of a ministry, government department or the European Commission.

Planned: no current model is stated and adopted text, such as a cabinet decision or an adopted plan, sets up the staffing in the future; a proposal gives not published, with the proposal quoted in the notes with its source and date.

Not published: searched and no source states it, with a dated search log of each query, its language and the sources read; it never means that the institute has no staff. Where a newer official source contradicts an older one, the newer one decides and the notes record both. Notes also record secondments, concurrent posts, partners and contractors beside the main model.

Source preference

Source preference: for a claim about a jurisdiction or its institute, cite that jurisdiction's own primary source first, in any language. Add an English-language source as a second citation where one exists, so readers can check it; it may come from another jurisdiction and does not replace the domestic primary source. Rely on an account from another country alone only when no source from the jurisdiction itself was found or retrieved, and name who reported the claim in its description. Quote the primary text itself, in its original language: the issuer's own page or document, not a press report, feed item, aggregator or translation of it. When the issuer's page cannot be retrieved, quote an archived copy of that same page and say so in the access note; cite a secondary account only when no primary text could be retrieved. Source origin identifies the issuer's country or international body, not the language or hosting server. Unknown origin is left null and reported for review, as are records with no citation from the jurisdiction itself.

Source titles

Source titles: keep the recorded original title. A web page's title is its own title or heading, not the site or publisher name that its HTML title may give alone. Add a faithful English translation when the title is non-English, including mixed-language titles; omit it for titles already in English and for proper names or acronyms alone. Source language describes the document read, not necessarily its title. A translation adds no dates, legal status or other claims that are not in the original, and is not presented as an official English title unless verified.

Consistency checks

Consistency checks: where the same date or figure appears in several records, a check confirms it matches everywhere; it does not test whether the source is right.

Quotation checks

Quotation checks: each quotation is matched word for word against our saved copy of its source; whether it supports the claim is judged in review.

Private evidence copies

Private evidence copies are kept for verification outside the repository. Complete HTML responses or PDF bytes are saved with the retrieval timestamp; rendered text from a third-party reader service is used when the original content is unavailable. Complete cited office documents and data archives are also kept. Published source snapshot metadata contains only the retrieval timestamp, the SHA-256 checksum of the copy and, when the copy has text, a versioned hash of its normalised main text; it never contains the copies or private paths. A later retrieval replaces the copy only when a quotation bound to the source matches the cited copy but no longer matches the new copy, when a bound quotation matches the new copy but not the cited copy, or when the cited copy is no longer held; for a source without bound quotations, only when its main text changed or cannot be compared. Other changes are reported for review and keep the cited copy. Public archive links use Wayback, with archive.today (archive.ph) as a fallback when a usable capture is found. Login, error and empty archive pages are rejected; unavailable sources stay recorded as explicit access limitations. We save a copy of each source as we read it; the copy shows what the source said, not that it is true. A Wayback viewer is checked through its raw document replay before judging the capture. New or replaced public archive links must match the recorded source URL or a documented redirect and have substantive matching content. A www host variant may be accepted without a recorded redirect only when its validated document bytes exactly match the original. Existing declared links that cannot be verified are kept, with their limitations reported and without any claim that their target or content has been verified. Captures shown to be the wrong document, a login page or an error page are removed. Retrieval timestamps keep their recorded precision and UTC offset.

Closest candidate

Closest candidate: a jurisdiction with no institute identified may record one body with a model-level evaluation, testing or safety role, or a self-described qualifying function. Certification, performance testing and protection of AI models against attacks can meet this narrower related-body filter without qualifying for institute status. Stage describes the body, not the jurisdiction's status; pending extensions to an existing body are stated in its note. The gap is remit when a government act creates, plans, proposes or recognises the body but does not give it a qualifying role, and designation when the body does qualifying work, self-described or documented, without a government act concerning it. When both apply, remit takes precedence. The note states the evidence and its limits. Other related bodies are not recorded in this field.

None identified entries

None identified entries: each state was searched in English and in at least one of its national or official languages, English counting for both where it is official, for a body its government has tasked with testing general-purpose or frontier AI models or named its AI safety or security institute; the entry names the languages searched. The bodies it lists never count as institutes: the closest candidate, AI Act authorities grouped by function, and other bodies. English names are our own faithful translations unless the body publishes its own English name, with the original shown too. The European Commission's contact list of 7 September 2026 marks some AI Act authorities as pending final adoption; for those states the national sources cited give the actual designation status.

Currency and rounding

Currency and rounding: Budgets published in other currencies are converted to euros at the ECB's annual-average rates for 2025, without inflation or purchasing-power adjustment. Every amount in a currency with an ECB reference rate is also shown in euros at these rates, followed by the stated amount, as in ~EUR 52m (JPY 8.8bn), whether or not it is compared; the conversion does not make an amount comparable. Amounts in a currency for which the ECB publishes no reference rate, such as the Kenyan shilling, are shown in that currency only, are not converted to euros and are never compared. Budget and staff figures are shown at two significant figures, and ~ marks a figure that is converted or changed by rounding, except the medians in the counts row. The exact values are in the downloads.

Budget display

Budget display: every funding record states whether it covers the whole institute, a component or funding that is not institute-specific. Use the comparable whole-institute annual figure selected under Annual funding for both the cards and the funding comparison. If none qualifies, use the latest adopted or announced whole-institute funding amount, including one-off and multi-year allocations; if there is none, use the latest whole-institute proposal. Exclude only records whose decision date lies wholly after the dataset date; an allocation already announced for a future funding period stays eligible. Rank eligible records by decision date when published, otherwise by the end of the sourced funding period, newest first; records with neither date follow those with one. Components and records without an amount cannot supply this fallback. Show the value with its period, commitment and scope qualifications. If no own funding qualifies, show Not published. An institute with own funding but no compared figure is shown with its Budget value and without a bar. Parent-body, host-programme, wider-ministry and broader-purpose amounts, including a body's funding before its designation, appear only as separately labelled Related funding, and stay in the downloads.

Budget ratios

Budget ratios: per person and share of GDP are computed only for the annual figure compared under Annual funding, and only for states; international organisations, such as the EU, and institutes without a compared figure have none. Per person divides that figure in euros by the state's population as recorded (see the population rules). Share of GDP divides it by the World Bank's GDP in current national currency for 2025, the year of the exchange rates. When the budget is in that currency no exchange rate enters, otherwise both are converted at the ECB 2025 rates. Both are computed from unrounded amounts and keep the figure's qualifier, so an upper bound stays an upper bound and rounds up. They scale the figure and do not adjust it for purchasing power or for differences in what the figure covers. The population and GDP figures are for 2025, while the compared figure is for a funding period overlapping the reference year, which may be a fiscal year, a current-year allocation or a multi-year mean. The median annual budget in the counts row is taken over the compared figures as stated; when any of them is an upper bound, the median is an upper bound too, shown with "up to" and rounded up.
